# ZestIQ organic growth playbook

Updated October 6, 2026.

- Vercel Analytics, previous 7 days: 24 visitors, 58 page views, 63% bounce rate, and 1 recorded Google referrer.
- The strongest non-brand opportunity is restaurant inventory and food-cost software. The strongest existing page is `/restaurant-food-cost-software`, which has already appeared around page one for some searches.
- ZestIQ's brand query is visible near the top, but generic discovery is still early.

- Real ZestIQ product screenshots and clearer evaluation criteria on the buyer's guide.
- A weekly restaurant inventory checklist built around an operator's actual count-to-order workflow.
- A supplier invoice and price-change tracking guide.

- Conversion events for starting, completing, and failing a demo request.
- Updated sitemap entries, crawlable content, location signals, titles, descriptions, and structured data.

## Google Business Profile

Create one profile only if ZestIQ is eligible under Google's current rules.

1. Use the business name `ZestIQ` without adding keywords.
2. Choose the closest software-company category available. Do not describe ZestIQ as a restaurant.
3. If customers do not visit a staffed public office, configure a service-area business and hide the address. Do not use a virtual office or expose a home address.
4. Use `https://zestiq.ca/restaurant-inventory-software-burlington` as the landing page for a Burlington-focused profile, if Google permits it.
5. Add the real business phone, hours, logo, product screenshots, and a concise services description.
6. Ask real users and pilot customers for honest reviews. Never write reviews for them or offer an incentive tied to a positive rating.

Suggested services: restaurant inventory management, food-cost tracking, invoice scanning, recipe costing, supplier ordering, beverage costing, waste tracking, and multi-location reporting.

## Legitimate backlink targets

Prioritize listings and partnerships that a restaurant buyer would actually trust.

1. Burlington Chamber of Commerce and other legitimate local business directories.
2. Burlington and Halton business, technology, and hospitality associations for which ZestIQ is genuinely eligible.
3. Restaurants Canada and Ontario hospitality associations where a vendor profile, member directory, article, or webinar is available.
4. POS and accounting partner directories after the integration is functional and approved by the partner.
5. Restaurant consultants, bookkeepers, fractional controllers, suppliers, and hospitality agencies that can review or refer ZestIQ.
6. Customer websites and case studies, but only with the customer's permission and a real implementation story.

Avoid mass directory submissions, purchased link packages, reciprocal-link networks, automated Google clicks, and keyword-stuffed guest posts.

## Outreach angles that earn links and conversations

- Give Burlington restaurant operators the weekly inventory checklist as a useful resource, not as a disguised link request.
- Offer a co-written case study to the first successful pilot customer.
- Offer bookkeeping and restaurant consulting partners a practical invoice-price-change guide they can share with clients.
- Pitch an educational local session: “How independent restaurants can catch supplier price increases before month end.”
- Invite POS and accounting providers to verify ZestIQ's integration copy before being included on the integrations page.

## Review request

Send this only to a real user who has experienced the product:

> Thanks again for trying ZestIQ. If it has made inventory, ordering, or food-cost review easier, would you be comfortable sharing an honest Google review? A sentence about what changed in your weekly workflow would be especially helpful. No pressure at all—specific feedback, positive or critical, helps us improve.

## Publishing cadence

Publish one substantial, experience-based item each week. Improve an existing page when there is no genuinely useful new subject.

- Week 1: Weekly restaurant inventory checklist — published in this update.
- Week 2: How to track supplier price changes from restaurant invoices — published in this update.
- Week 3: A real pilot case study with baseline, workflow, and measured result.
- Week 4: Restaurant count-sheet template with storage-area examples.
- Week 5: Food-cost variance troubleshooting guide using a worked example.
- Week 6: POS-to-inventory integration guide that clearly states which connections are live, planned, or manual.

Every page should answer one buyer question, show original evidence or experience, link to the next useful page, and offer one clear action.

## Weekly measurement

Review Search Console every Monday using a 28-day comparison, because daily ranking changes are noisy and Search Console data can lag.

- Non-brand impressions and clicks.
- Queries moving into positions 11–30, which are the best near-term optimization targets.
- Page-level click-through rate after title changes.
- Demo form starts, successful submissions, and failures in Vercel Analytics.
- Qualified demo requests, not just raw traffic.
- Referring domains and the specific relationships that earned them.

## Realistic expectation

These improvements can help Google understand and trust the site, but no legitimate change can guarantee page one on a particular day. Technical changes can be crawled in days; meaningful movement for competitive generic terms usually requires repeated proof, links, engagement, and customer evidence over weeks or months. The leading indicator is growing non-brand impressions, followed by positions 11–30, then qualified clicks and demos.
